## 3. KEY FINANCIAL METRICS

### 3.1 Customer Economics

#### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

```
Total Year 1 marketing & sales spend:   ₱180,000
New customers added (Year 1):
  Cebu: 72 - 50 = 22 new customers
  Bohol: 25 new customers
  Total: 47 new customers

CAC calculation:
  ₱180,000 / 47 customers = ₱3,829 per customer
  
Breakdown by channel (estimate):
  Referrals (low-cost):              ₱500/customer
  Partnerships:                      ₱1,200/customer
  Direct sales:                      ₱3,000/customer
  Organic/word-of-mouth:             ₱0/customer
  Paid ads (low spend Year 1):       ₱2,000/customer (if used)
```

**Observation:** CAC is high relative to monthly fee (₱2,500–₱3,000). This requires high customer lifetime value (CLV) to be profitable.

#### Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

```
Average monthly revenue per customer:   ₱3,000 (blended)
Average customer lifetime:              18 months (estimate)
  - Assumption: 5% monthly churn
  - Formula: 1 / 0.05 = 20 months average lifetime
  - Conservative: 18 months used

CLV calculation:
  ₱3,000 × 18 months = ₱54,000 per customer

Payback period (CAC / Monthly revenue):
  ₱3,829 / ₱3,000 = 1.3 months
  - Very healthy (payback in ~1 month)
  - Implies profitable from early months if churn low

LTV:CAC ratio:
  ₱54,000 / ₱3,829 = 14.1x
  - Excellent (benchmark: 3x+ is healthy)
  - Indicates very profitable unit economics once scaled
```

**Implication:** If customer retention is good (5%+ monthly churn sustainable), the business unit economics are **highly profitable**. The Year 1 loss is temporary, funded by customer acquisition investment.

### 3.2 Operational Metrics

#### Cost per Customer Served

```
Total operating expenses (Year 1):      ₱3,455,500
Average customers served (during Year):
  Cebu: (50 + 72) / 2 = 61 avg
  Bohol: (0 + 25) / 2 = 12.5 avg
  Total: 73.5 customers average

Cost per customer per month:
  ₱3,455,500 / 12 months / 73.5 customers = ₱3,913/customer/month
  
Monthly fee per customer:              ₱3,000 average
Gross margin per customer:             ₱3,000 - ₱3,913 = (₱913) loss

Wait—this means we're unprofitable per customer. Let me recalculate...

Actually, the issue is that operating expenses include overhead (office, management, tech),
not purely per-customer service cost. Better metric:

COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) per customer:
  - Direct payroll (support):         ₱500/customer/month
  - Cloud hosting:                    ₱100/customer/month
  - Payment processing:               ₱50/customer/month
  ────────────────────────────────────
  Total COGS:                         ₱650/customer/month

Contribution margin per customer:
  ₱3,000 revenue - ₱650 COGS = ₱2,350 contribution/customer/month
  
Contribution margin %:
  ₱2,350 / ₱3,000 = 78% (excellent)

Operating expenses (non-COGS):        ₱2,805,500 / 12 = ₱233,791/month
Operating margin:
  (₱2,350 × 73.5 customers) - ₱233,791 = ₱172,698 - ₱233,791 = (₱61,093)/month

This matches the monthly loss in P&L (≈ -₱50k–₱90k/month average)
```

**Insight:** Unit economics are healthy (78% contribution margin), but fixed overhead not yet covered by customer base. As customer count grows (100+ customers), fixed overhead gets absorbed, turning profitable.

## 8. FUNDING & CAPITAL STRUCTURE

### 8.1 Funding Options

**Total capital required (Year 1):** ₱1,100,000 – ₱1,200,000

#### Option A: Venture Capital (Series A)

**Pros:**
- Large capital injection (₱10M–₱50M possible)
- Experienced investors provide guidance/network
- Enable aggressive growth (hire more, spend more on marketing)
- Founder credibility/validation

**Cons:**
- Dilution of founder equity (20–40% typical)
- Investor control/board seat
- Pressure to grow fast (may sacrifice profitability)
- Complex legal process (2–4 months)

**Timing:** June–August 2026 (before Bohol launch needed)

**Valuation scenario:**
- Pre-money valuation: ₱100M (based on Cebu traction)
- Series A size: ₱20M
- Post-money valuation: ₱120M
- Founder dilution: 20M / 120M = 16.7% dilution

#### Option B: Founder Savings / Loan

**Pros:**
- No dilution
- Founder retains 100% control
- Quick execution
- Simpler governance

**Cons:**
- Limited capital (likely <₱5M personal savings)
- Personal financial risk
- Slower growth (bootstrap model)
- May require external funding later anyway

**Timing:** Immediate (June 2026)

**Viability:** If founder has ₱1.2M available, can self-fund Year 1. Typical bootstrap timeline to profitability: 18–24 months.

#### Option C: Bank Credit Line

**Pros:**
- Moderate capital (₱2M–₱5M typical)
- Low dilution (secured loan, not equity)
- Flexible drawdown
- Tax-deductible interest

**Cons:**
- Requires profitable revenue history (hard for early-stage)
- Personal guarantee typical
- Debt service obligation
- Risk if business underperforms

**Timing:** June–July 2026 (approval process 2–4 weeks)

**Typical terms:**
- Amount: ₱1.5M credit line
- Interest rate: 8–12% per annum
- Tenor: 2–3 years
- Repayment: ₱60k–₱80k per month (after break-even)

#### Option D: Hybrid (Bootstrap + Strategic Investor)

**Approach:**
- Founder provides ₱200k–₱300k initial capital
- Seek ₱500k–₱800k from angel investor or micro-VC
- Total ₱700k–₱1.1M sufficient for Year 1

**Pros:**
- Moderate dilution (10–15% vs. 20%+ Series A)
- Shared risk with strategic investor
- Access to network/expertise
- Faster than Series A

**Cons:**
- Limited capital for aggressive growth
- Smaller investor may have less support
- Multiple stakeholders (founder + investor)

**Timing:** July–August 2026 (4–8 weeks to close)

### 8.2 Recommended Funding Path

**Recommendation: Option D (Hybrid) for Year 1**

**Rationale:**
1. Founder contributes ₱300k (demonstrates commitment)
2. Seek ₱800k from angel/early-stage VC (25–30% dilution target)
3. Total ₱1.1M covers Year 1 expansion with buffer

- Valuation likely ₱500M+ (much higher than current ₱100M)
