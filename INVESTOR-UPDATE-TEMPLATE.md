# ElSpa Investor Update Template
## Monthly Board Update & Investor Communications

---

## Overview

This document provides a template for monthly investor updates post-Series A. Regular, transparent communication builds investor confidence and helps detect issues early.

**Cadence:**
- **Monthly**: Concise 2-3 page investor update (email format)
- **Quarterly**: Detailed board packages (5-10 pages, with detailed metrics)
- **Annual**: Investor letter (2-4 pages, strategic reflection)

**Audience:**
- Series A investor(s)
- Angel investors
- Board members
- Optional: Key employees (transparency)

---

## SECTION 1: MONTHLY INVESTOR UPDATE TEMPLATE

### Subject Line

```
ElSpa Monthly Update - [Month] 2026 | MRR: $[X] | Status: On Track
```

**Good subject lines:**
- "ElSpa Monthly Update - June 2026 | MRR: $15K | Hiring CTO"
- "ElSpa Monthly Update - July 2026 | MRR: $25K | Product Launch"
- "ElSpa Monthly Update - August 2026 | MRR: $35K | North America Entry"

**Avoid:**
- Vague: "ElSpa Update"
- Negative: "ElSpa Monthly Update - [Month] | Missed Targets"
- Too detailed: "ElSpa Update - Detailed Breakdown of Q3 Performance vs. Projections"

## SECTION 2: QUARTERLY BOARD PACKAGE TEMPLATE

**Quarterly board packages are more detailed.** Typically 5-10 pages, including:

1. **Letter from CEO** (1 page strategic narrative)
2. **Financial summary** (1 page P&L + balance sheet)
3. **Metric dashboard** (1 page KPIs)
4. **Customer & revenue analysis** (1 page: cohort retention, churn, CAC)
5. **Product roadmap** (1 page: what shipped, what's next)
6. **Market & competitive update** (1 page)
7. **Team & hiring** (0.5 pages)
8. **Risk assessment** (0.5 pages)
9. **Appendix** (detailed financials, metrics, customer references)

**Timing:**
- Sent 1 week before board meeting
- Allows board members to review before meeting
- Board meeting typically 2 hours (1 hour review, 1 hour discussion)

## SECTION 4: COMMUNICATION BEST PRACTICES

### Tone & Voice

**DO:**
- Be honest about challenges (investors respect transparency)
- Use specific numbers (not "growing fast" but "88% MoM growth")
- Celebrate wins but acknowledge risks
- Write in clear, jargon-free language
- Use analogies/storytelling to make complex ideas simple

**DON'T:**
- Over-promise and under-deliver
- Hide bad news (it always comes out)
- Use vague language ("significant progress")
- Make excuses (take accountability)
- Change story month-to-month

### Timing & Frequency

**Monthly updates:**
- Send on 1st business day of next month
- Keep to 3 pages max (busy investors won't read 10)
- Include headline metrics in subject line
- Assume 5-10 minute read time

**Quarterly board meetings:**
- Schedule 3 months in advance (coordination nightmare otherwise)
- Typically 2 hours (1 hour review + 1 hour discussion)
- Send board materials 1 week before
- Record decisions and action items

**Annual letter:**
- Send after fiscal year-end
- Reflect on what worked, what didn't
- Set context for next year's strategy
- More personal than monthly updates

### Metrics to Always Track

**Growth metrics:**
- MRR / ARR
- # of customers
- Customer acquisition rate

**Retention metrics:**
- Churn rate
- NPS
- Net Retention Rate (expansion revenue vs. churn)

**Unit economics:**
- CAC (customer acquisition cost)
- LTV (lifetime value)
- Payback period
- Gross margin

**Operational metrics:**
- Headcount
- Burn rate
- Runway
- Key product metrics (uptime, feature adoption)

**Cohort analysis:**
- Customer cohorts by acquisition month
- Cohort retention by month
- CAC by channel

### Red Flags Investors Watch For

**Watch out for these patterns:**

1. **Declining growth rate** (50% → 30% → 15%)
   - Signals: Market saturation, loss of PMF, competitive pressure

2. **Increasing churn** (0% → 2% → 5%)
   - Signals: Product issues, customer fit problem, competitive loss

3. **Declining NPS** (82 → 70 → 60)
   - Signals: Customer satisfaction issues, feature regressions

4. **Rising CAC** ($800 → $1,200 → $2,000)
   - Signals: Market saturation, increased competition, harder customer acquisition

5. **Changing narrative** (month 1: "SE Asia focus", month 2: "Actually, US expansion first")
   - Signals: Founder confusion, lack of conviction, operational issues

**Best practice:** If you see a red flag, acknowledge it immediately and explain mitigation. Investors respect transparency and rapid course correction.

## SECTION 6: COMMON INVESTOR QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

### Q: Why are your metrics different this month vs. last month?

**A:** Always explain variance. Example:

"MRR declined from $15K to $14K this month due to [customer #3 churn] which we mitigated 
by closing [new customer] mid-month. We expect to be back to $16K+ by next month."

### Q: Can you break down CAC by channel?

**A:** Yes, provide channel breakdown monthly.

"Current CAC: $800 blended, but varies by channel:
- Referral: $0 (40% of customers)
- Outbound sales: $1,200 (40% of customers)
- Inbound/content: $1,500 (20% of customers)"

### Q: How are you measuring NPS?

**A:** Explain methodology.

"We survey each customer quarterly with standard NPS question: 'How likely are you to 
recommend ElSpa to other spas?' Responses: 9-10 = promoter, 7-8 = passive, 0-6 = detractor. 
Score = (Promoters - Detractors) / Total × 100."

### Q: What's your churn looking like beyond 3 months?

**A:** Track cohort retention closely.

"Feb cohort (oldest): 4 months retained, still active, expanding usage.
Mar cohort: 3 months retained, all active.
April cohort: 2 months retained, early days but strong engagement.
We don't yet have a customer cohort beyond 6 months, but trajectory suggests 
<5% annual churn based on engagement metrics."
