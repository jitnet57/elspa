## SECTION 2: KEY TERM SHEET TERMS

### Series A Term Sheet Structure

**Standard term sheet covers:**
1. **Valuation & sizing**
2. **Liquidation preferences**
3. **Board composition**
4. **Protective provisions**
5. **Anti-dilution clauses**
6. **Voting agreements**

Let me break down each.

---

### Term 1: Liquidation Preference

**What it is:**
In an exit, who gets paid first?

**Types:**
1. **Non-participating preferred**: Investor gets return OR equity percentage, whichever is higher
2. **Participating preferred**: Investor gets return AND equity percentage
3. **Capped participating**: Investor gets return + equity, but capped at 2-3x investment

**Market standard:** Non-participating (1x return, unless company appreciates significantly)

**ElSpa negotiation:**
- Offer: 1x non-participating preferred (investor-friendly, but standard)
- If pressed: 1x participating with 2x cap (middle ground)
- Avoid: 2x non-participating (punitive to founder, implies low confidence)

**Impact analysis:**
```
Scenario: Company sells for $100M

Founder stake: 75% (owned $75M)
Investor stake: 25% held as Series A Preferred

Non-participating (1x):
- Investor gets: $2.5M (1x investment) or 25% = $25M (whichever is higher)
- Since $25M > $2.5M, investor takes $25M
- Founder gets: $75M
- Total: $100M ✓

Participating (1x):
- Investor gets: $2.5M + 25% of proceeds
- = $2.5M + $25M = $27.5M (investor favored)
- Founder gets: $72.5M (founder disadvantaged)
- Total: $100M ✓

Participating (1x capped at 2x):
- Investor return is capped at $5M (2x investment)
- Investor gets: min($5M + $25M, 2x $2.5M) = min($27.5M, $5M) = $5M
- Founder gets: $95M (founder heavily favored)
- This is rare at Series A
```

**Recommendation for ElSpa:**
- **Accept 1x non-participating** (standard, fair to both parties)
- **Negotiate against 2x participating** (punitive to founder)

### Term 2: Board Composition

**What it is:**
Who sits on the board of directors?

**Typical Series A:**
- **Founder/CEO**: 1 seat
- **Series A investor**: 1 seat
- **Independent director**: 1 seat
- **Total: 3 seats**

**ElSpa current:** 1 seat (founder only)

**Post-Series A structure:**
- Kang Jichul (CEO/Founder)
- Series A investor representative
- Independent director (recruited from advisors/network)

**Independent director candidates:**
- Spa industry veteran (existing advisor)
- SaaS operator (Stripe alum, existing advisor)
- Serial entrepreneur (to be recruited)

**Why this matters:**
- Investor gets visibility into company operations
- Independent director brings outside perspective
- Founder retains control (3-person board, 1-1-1)
- Escalates to 5-person board at Series B (usually)

**Recommendation for ElSpa:**
- **Accept 3-person board** with investor seat (market standard)
- **Negotiate for independent chair** (balances power)
- Ensure independent director has startup experience

---

### Term 3: Anti-Dilution Protection

**What it is:**
If we raise Series B at lower valuation, how does Series A protect themselves?

**Types:**
1. **Broad-based weighted average**: Most common, least punitive
2. **Narrow-based weighted average**: More protective to early investors
3. **Full ratchet**: Most punitive (uncommon, deal-breaker for founders)

**Example:**
Series A invested $2.5M at $7.5M pre-money (25% stake).
Series B raises at $5M pre-money (down-round).

**Broad-based weighted average:**
- Series A stake increases to ~30% (adjustment for down-round)
- Founder diluted, but not catastrophically
- New investor gets ~20% stake

**Full ratchet (avoid at all costs):**
- Series A reverts to old price, gets massive dilution adjustment
- Series A could end up owning 40%+ (punitive to founder)
- Deal breaker

**Recommendation for ElSpa:**
- **Accept broad-based weighted average** (fair, market standard)
- **Explicitly exclude full ratchet** (make it a deal-breaker if insisted)
- **Negotiate for no anti-dilution if we do well** (if we raise at higher valuation, no adjustment needed)

---

### Term 4: Protective Provisions

**What it is:**
What decisions require investor approval?

**Standard protective provisions:**
1. **M&A or liquidation** (selling company, raising above X%, etc.)
2. **Changing board size or composition**
3. **Changing terms of preferred stock**
4. **Issuing new stock classes** (above option pool size)
5. **Changing business direction** (material pivot)

**ElSpa position:**
- **Accept 1-5** (standard, reasonable protections)
- **Negotiate against** overly broad definitions
- Example: "Material pivot" should be defined narrowly (not adding a new feature)

**Red flag terms to avoid:**
- "Any decision >$100K requires investor approval" (too granular, slows operations)
- "Any hire above VP-level requires approval" (slows hiring)
- "Any partnership over $50K requires approval" (slows business development)

**Recommendation for ElSpa:**
- **Accept big decisions** (M&A, fundraising, board composition)
- **Negotiate against operational decisions** (hiring, contracts, partnerships)

---

### Term 5: Information Rights

**What it is:**
What financial/operational information must we provide to investors?

**Standard information rights:**
1. **Monthly**: Financial statements (revenue, burn, cash position)
2. **Quarterly**: Board packages (financials, metrics, narrative)
3. **Annual**: Audited financials (optional at Series A, common at Series B)
4. **As-requested**: Access to cap table, customer list, payroll (for due diligence)

**ElSpa commitment:**
- Monthly: P&L and cash position (easy, we track anyway)
- Quarterly: Board packages (new process to implement)
- Annual: Financial review (not full audit, unless requested)

**Recommendation for ElSpa:**
- **Accept monthly + quarterly reporting** (standard, doable)
- **Negotiate against excessive disclosure** (limit to business operations, exclude confidential customer data)

---

### Term 6: Pro-Rata Rights

**What it is:**
If we do Series B, do existing investors get to participate?

**Standard:** Investors get right to maintain their ownership percentage in future rounds

**Example:**
Series A investor owns 25% after Series A.
Series B raises $5M at $20M pre-money (new valuation).
To maintain 25%, Series A investor would need to invest $1.25M in Series B.

**But:** Pro-rata rights are optional, not required.

**ElSpa position:**
- **Accept pro-rata rights** (standard, fair to early investors)
- Ensures early believers can continue participating
- Doesn't obligate them to invest more

---

### Term 7: Drag-Along Rights

**What it is:**
If founders want to sell company, can they force investors to participate?

**Standard:** Yes, founders can drag minority shareholders along in sale

**Example:**
- Series A investor owns 25%
- Series B investor owns 30%
- Founder owns 45%

If founder finds buyer at $100M, can founder force Series A/B to sell? Yes (drag-along).

**Purpose:** Prevents a single shareholder from blocking a good exit

**ElSpa position:**
- **Accept drag-along rights** (standard, necessary for exits)
- Ensures we're not held hostage by minority investor

## SECTION 4: KEY NEGOTIATION POINTS

### Points to Accept (Market Standard)

1. **25% investor stake** (reasonable for $2.5M seed round)
2. **1x non-participating liquidation preference** (standard, fair)
3. **3-person board** with investor seat (market norm)
4. **Pro-rata rights** (standard, fair to investors)
5. **1-year vesting** on founder equity (ensures commitment)
6. **Drag-along rights** (necessary for exits)
7. **Anti-dilution (broad-based weighted)** (fair, prevents runaway down-rounds)

### Points to Negotiate

1. **Information rights**
   - Accept monthly financials + quarterly board packages
   - Negotiate against "any material contract >$50K requires approval"

2. **Protective provisions**
   - Accept M&A, fundraising, board changes
   - Negotiate against operational decisions (hiring, partnerships)

3. **Board seat**
   - Ideally: Investor rep on board, but CEO is chair (CEO controls agenda)
   - Negotiate: Independent director must have startup experience

4. **Option pool**
   - 10% is standard (don't accept <8% or >15%)
   - Clarify: Is pool pre-Series A or post-Series A size?

### Points to Avoid (Red Flags)

1. **2x participating liquidation preference** (punitive, deal-breaker)
2. **Full ratchet anti-dilution** (catastrophic in down-round, deal-breaker)
3. **Investor must approve any hire >Director level** (operational control)
4. **Investor must approve any contract >$10K** (micro-manages business)
5. **Liquidation preference on top of equity** (double-dipping)

## SECTION 6: NEGOTIATION SCENARIO & TRADE-OFFS

### If Investor Demands Higher Valuation

**Investor's opening**: $12M pre-money (implied 5x Year 1 ARR)

**Your position:**
- Start at $7.5M pre-money (3.1x Year 1 ARR)
- If investor pushes, can offer $8-9M pre-money
- This is still lower than comparable Series A rounds

**Trade-off:**
- If investor wants $10M pre-money: Accept 20% stake instead of 25%
- Keeps your dilution manageable while meeting investor halfway

**Math:**
- $2.5M / $10M post-money = 20% stake (investor favors this)
- Pre-money would be $7.5M (acceptable to you)
- Both sides win

---

### If Investor Demands Larger Check Size

**Investor's opening**: $5M (instead of $2.5M)

**Your position:**
- Only need $2.5M to reach profitability
- Extra capital = excess dilution
- Prefer focused allocation

**Trade-off:**
- If investor insists on $5M: Negotiate for lower valuation ($12M post instead of $15M)
- Or: Accept $5M at higher valuation ($15M post, which implies $10M pre)

**Recommendation:**
- **Prefer $2.5M at $7.5M pre** (less dilution, proven execution with smaller capital)
- Easier to raise Series B from position of strength (profitable + growing)

---

### If Investor Demands 2x Liquidation Preference

**Investor's opening**: 2x non-participating preferred

**This is a RED FLAG.** Signals investor doesn't believe in upside.

**Your position:**
- 1x non-participating is market standard
- 2x is unusual for post-PMF companies
- If investor wants 2x, they're implying low confidence
- This is a deal-breaker for us

**What to do:**
- Push back firmly
- Say: "1x is what we're comfortable with; if you want downside protection, we're the wrong investment"
- If investor won't budge: Walk away (not the right investor)

**Why it matters:**
- In a $100M exit, 2x preference means investor gets $5M instead of $2.5M
- Founder gets $95M instead of $97.5M (small in percentage terms)
- But it signals investor's lack of confidence, which is a bad omen
