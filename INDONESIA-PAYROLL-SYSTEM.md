# Indonesia Payroll System & Labor Compliance Guide

**Date:** 2026-05-29  
**Project:** ElSpa Go-To-Market (Indonesia)  
**Prepared by:** Compliance & Finance Team

## Executive Summary

Indonesia has a comprehensive labor law framework overseen by the Ministry of Labor (Kementerian Ketenagakerjaan) with mandatory social security contributions, health insurance, income tax withholding, and generous leave entitlements. Compliance is strict with penalties for violations. Payroll operations require integration with BPJS (social security authority) and tax authority (DJP). This document outlines all statutory requirements for operating in Indonesia.

---

## 1. Indonesian Labor Law Overview

### 1.1 Key Legal Framework

**Primary Laws & Regulations**

| Law/Regulation | Year | Key Provisions |
|----------------|------|-----------------|
| **Law No. 13 of 2003** | 2003 | Labor Law (fundamental employment rights, wages, working hours, termination) |
| **Law No. 24 of 2011** | 2011 | Social Security Law (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan & Kesehatan requirements) |
| **PP No. 78 of 2015** | 2015 | Minimum Wage Regulation (annual wage floor by province) |
| **PP No. 46 of 2011** | 2011 | Income Tax (PPh 21 withholding requirements) |
| **Ministry Regulation No. 1 of 2023** | 2023 | Severance & Leave Entitlements (updated enforcement) |
| **Regional Regulations** | Annual | Provincial minimum wage decrees (Jakarta, Bali vary) |

**Governing Authorities:**
- **Ministry of Labor (Kemenkes):** Enforce labor standards, licensing, dispute resolution
- **Tax Authority (DJP):** Collect PPh 21 (income tax), corporate tax, VAT
- **BPJS Ketenagakerjaan:** Manage social security (work accident, disability, death, pension)
- **BPJS Kesehatan:** Manage health insurance coverage
- **Regional Labor Office (Dinas Tenaga Kerja):** Provincial-level enforcement & inspections

### 1.2 Employment Contract Requirements

**Mandatory Contract Elements (Undang-Undang No. 13/2003):**

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|------------|
| **Contract Type** | Fixed-term (PKWT ≤2 years, renewable once) OR Indefinite (PKWTT) |
| **Job Title & Duties** | Clear description of role & responsibilities |
| **Compensation & Benefits** | Salary, allowances, bonuses (must not fall below minimum wage) |
| **Working Hours** | 40 hours/week (max 8 hours/day) or sector-specific (tourism: 7–8 hours) |
| **Probation Period** | Max 3 months (for fixed-term); can deduct 25% salary during probation |
| **Termination Clause** | Severance terms, notice period (≥30 days) |
| **Dispute Resolution** | Reference to labor court or mediation |
| **Language** | Must be in Indonesian; foreign language copies optional but helpful |

**Key Note:** Contracts must NOT contain clauses that waive employee rights (e.g., waiving severance, health insurance, minimum wage).

## 2. Mandatory Social Security System (BPJS)

### 2.1 BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (Social Security for Work)

**Program Overview & Mandatory Coverage**

| Program | Coverage | Employee Contribution | Employer Contribution | Total Rate |
|---------|----------|----------------------|----------------------|------------|
| **Work Accident Insurance (JKK)** | Covers work-related injuries, disabilities, death | 0% | 0.24–0.74% of gross salary | 0.24–0.74% |
| **Death Benefit (JKM)** | Death (work or non-work), funeral assistance | 0% | 0.30% of gross salary | 0.30% |
| **Disability Benefit (JKD)** | Permanent disability (any cause) | 0% | 0.30% of gross salary | 0.30% |
| **Old Age Benefit (JHT)** | Pension at age 55, lump-sum or annuity | 2% of salary | 3.70% of salary | 5.70% |
| **Pension (Pensiun)** | Monthly pension post-retirement | 1% of salary | 2% of salary | 3% |
| **TOTAL EMPLOYER CONTRIBUTION** | — | 3% | 6.54–7.04% | 9.54–10.04% |

**BPJS Ketenagakerjaan Calculation Example (Monthly Salary IDR 5 million):**

| Component | Calculation | Amount (IDR) |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| Gross Salary | — | 5,000,000 |
| Employee Contribution (3%) | 5M × 3% | (150,000) |
| **Employee Salary After Deduction** | — | **4,850,000** |
| Employer Contribution (7.04%) | 5M × 7.04% | 352,000 |
| **Total BPJS Cost** | Employee + Employer | 502,000 |

**Administration:**
- Enrollment: PT must register with BPJS within 30 days of hiring
- Payment: Monthly, due by 15th of following month
- Penalties: Late payment incurs 2% penalty + interest (legal rate)
- Employer Liability: Responsible for both employee & employer contributions; non-payment can result in operational suspension

### 2.2 BPJS Kesehatan (Health Insurance)

**Program Overview & Mandatory Coverage**

| Program | Coverage | Employee Contribution | Employer Contribution | Total Rate |
|---------|----------|----------------------|----------------------|------------|
| **Health Insurance (Kesehatan)** | Outpatient, inpatient, emergency care, preventive | 1% of salary | 3–4% of salary | 4–5% |

**BPJS Kesehatan Calculation Example (Monthly Salary IDR 5 million):**

| Component | Calculation | Amount (IDR) |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| Gross Salary | — | 5,000,000 |
| Employee Contribution (1%) | 5M × 1% | (50,000) |
| **Employee Salary After Deduction** | — | **4,950,000** |
| Employer Contribution (4%) | 5M × 4% | 200,000 |
| **Total BPJS Kesehatan Cost** | Employee + Employer | 250,000 |

**Coverage & Beneficiaries:**
- Employee covered for all medical services
- Family members (spouse, children) eligible under family card (Kartu Keluarga)
- Coverage includes: doctor visits, hospital stays, pharmacy, diagnostic tests, maternity

**Administration:**
- Enrollment: Automatic upon joining BPJS Ketenagakerjaan
- Separate premium payments to BPJS Kesehatan
- Payment: Monthly, due by 10th of following month
- Claim Process: Beneficiary uses BPJS card at accredited hospitals/clinics

## 3. Income Tax Withholding (PPh 21)

### 3.1 PPh 21 (Personal Income Tax) Framework

**Overview:**
PPh 21 is mandatory income tax withheld by employers on employee salaries. Employees receive tax relief (PTKP - Personal Tax Allowance) and can deduct certain expenses.

**Personal Tax Allowance (PTKP) 2025–2026:**

| Status | Annual Allowance | Monthly Allowance |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Single (TK)** | IDR 54 million | IDR 4.5 million |
| **Married (K)** | IDR 58.5 million | IDR 4.875 million |
| **Married + 1 Child (K/1)** | IDR 63 million | IDR 5.25 million |
| **Married + 2 Children (K/2)** | IDR 67.5 million | IDR 5.625 million |
| **Married + 3 Children (K/3)** | IDR 72 million | IDR 6 million |

**Note:** PTKP is updated annually by tax authority (DJP). Above figures are 2025 estimates.

### 3.2 PPh 21 Calculation (Monthly)

**Step-by-Step Calculation Example:**

**Assumptions:**
- Monthly Salary: IDR 5 million
- Status: Single (TK)
- Employee Contributions: BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (3%) + BPJS Kesehatan (1%) = 4%

| Step | Calculation | Amount (IDR) |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **1. Gross Salary** | — | 5,000,000 |
| **2. Deductible Contributions** | | |
| BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (3%) | 5M × 3% | (150,000) |
| BPJS Kesehatan (1%) | 5M × 1% | (50,000) |
| **3. Taxable Income (Penghasilan Netto)** | Step 1 – Step 2 | 4,800,000 |
| **4. Monthly PTKP (Single)** | — | (4,500,000) |
| **5. Taxable Net Income (PKP)** | Step 3 – Step 4 | 300,000 |
| **6. Tax Rate** | 5% (bracket for IDR 0–50M annually) | 5% |
| **7. Monthly PPh 21** | Step 5 × 6% | **15,000** |

**Employee Net Pay:**
- Gross: IDR 5,000,000
- BPJS Ketenagakerjaan: (150,000)
- BPJS Kesehatan: (50,000)
- PPh 21: (15,000)
- **Net Pay: IDR 4,785,000**

### 3.3 PPh 21 Tax Brackets (Annual Income)

| Income Range | Tax Rate |
|--------------|----------|
| IDR 0–50 million | 5% |
| IDR 50–250 million | 15% |
| IDR 250–500 million | 25% |
| IDR 500M+ | 30% |

**Withholding Responsibility:**
- Employer must calculate & withhold PPh 21 from each paycheck
- Employer responsible for remitting to tax authority monthly
- Non-compliance incurs penalties (25% of unpaid tax + interest)

## 5. Bonuses & Special Allowances

### 5.1 THR (Tunjangan Hari Raya - 13th Month Bonus)

**Legal Requirement (Law No. 13/2003):**
- Mandatory for all employees with ≥1 year service
- Minimum: 1 month salary
- Payment timing: Latest by 7 days before Islamic Eid (Lebaran)
- Calculation: 1 month salary (based on last month worked + allowances)

**Annual Calendar (Islamic dates vary yearly):**
- **2026 Eid:** Approximately April 9–10
- **2027 Eid:** Approximately March 30–31
- **2028 Eid:** Approximately March 19–20

**Example Calculation (Employee with IDR 5M salary):**

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Base Salary | IDR 5,000,000 |
| Transportation Allowance | IDR 500,000 |
| Meal Allowance | IDR 300,000 |
| **THR (1 month)** | **IDR 5,800,000** |

**Eligibility:**
- ≥1 year continuous service
- Employees leaving before Eid: Prorated (12 months / number of months worked)
- Contract employees: Entitled if contract duration ≥1 year

**Employer Accounting:** Accrue monthly (1/12 of annual THR cost) for accounting/cash management.

---

### 5.2 Performance Bonus & Incentives

**Non-Mandatory Incentives (Company Policy):**
- Profit-sharing bonus: May be offered (10–15% of net profit typical in Indonesia)
- Performance bonus: Tied to KPIs (therapist utilization, customer satisfaction, retention)
- Attendance bonus: Incentivize zero-absence behavior (common in service industry)
- Referral bonus: Encourage staff recruitment of new therapists

**Tax Treatment:**
- Performance & attendance bonuses: Taxable as regular income (subject to PPh 21)
- Profit-sharing: May qualify for partial exemption if structured as formal profit-sharing plan (requires board approval)

**Recommended ElSpa Incentive Structure:**
- Base salary: IDR 5–8M (therapist level)
- Performance bonus: 5–10% of base (quarterly, based on customer ratings & repeat booking rate)
- Annual bonus (separate from THR): 0.5–1 month salary (discretionary, if company reaches profitability)

## 6. Leave Entitlements & Time Off

### 6.1 Annual Leave (Cuti Tahunan)

**Legal Requirement (Law No. 13/2003):**

| Service Duration | Annual Leave Entitlement |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Year 1–6** | 12 working days/year |
| **Year 7+** | 16 working days/year |

**Rules & Policies:**
- Accrues monthly (1 day per month for first 6 years)
- Carry-over: 8 days maximum to next year (remaining 4 days forfeit if not taken)
- Usage: Must be approved by employer; taken with mutual agreement
- Payment if unused: Upon termination, unused leave paid at regular daily rate
- Cannot be replaced with cash during employment (except upon separation)

**Example:**
- Employee with 3 years service: 12 days/year
- Taken: 10 days in year
- Carry-over: 2 days into next year (max 8; so 2 are carried)
- Remaining: 10 new days + 2 carried = 12 available next year

### 6.3 Sick & Emergency Leave

**Sick Leave (Cuti Sakit):**
- First 3 days: No medical certificate required
- Day 4+: Medical certificate (SKTT) required
- Duration: Typically unpaid (at employer discretion) or partial pay (50%)
- Accrual: 12 days/year (paid) per industry standard

**Emergency/Compassionate Leave:**
- Death of family member: 1–3 days (paid)
- Marriage: 2–3 days (paid)
- Childbirth (for female employee): 1.5 months (3 weeks before, 6 weeks after)
- Medical treatment: Per doctor's recommendation (paid, up to 30 days)

**Note:** Sick leave, emergency leave are typically paid (employer discretion); must be documented for accountability.

## 7. Termination & Severance

### 7.1 Severance Pay (Pesangon Atas Permohonan Sendiri)

**If Employee Resigns (Own Request):**

| Service Duration | Severance (months) | Service Allowance (months) | Total |
|------------------|-------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| < 1 year | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1–5 years | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 5–10 years | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| 10–15 years | 3 | 1 | 4 |
| 15+ years | 4 | 1 | 5 |

**Calculation Basis:** Last monthly salary (base + regular allowances, ex. overtime)

**Example (5-year employee, IDR 6M salary):**
- Severance: 2 months = IDR 12 million
- Service allowance: 1 month = IDR 6 million
- Total: IDR 18 million

### 7.2 Severance Pay (Employer Termination)

**If Employer Terminates Without Cause:**

| Service Duration | Severance (months) | Service Allowance (months) | Compensation (months) | Total |
|------------------|-------------------|---------------------------|----------------------|-------|
| < 1 year | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 1–5 years | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 5–10 years | 3 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| 10–15 years | 4 | 1 | 1 | 6 |
| 15+ years | 5 | 1 | 1 | 7 |

**Employer Termination Example (5-year employee, IDR 6M salary):**
- Severance: 2 months = IDR 12 million
- Service allowance: 1 month = IDR 6 million
- Compensation: 1 month = IDR 6 million
- Total: IDR 24 million

**Conditions for "Without Cause" (Justifiable Termination - no severance above):**
- Serious misconduct (theft, violence, dishonesty)
- Abandonment (3+ consecutive days without notice)
- Gross insubordination
- Repeated policy violations after warnings

---

### 7.3 Termination Procedures & Notice

**Notice Period (Undang-Undang No. 13/2003):**
- Employer → Employee: 30 days (or pay 30 days salary in lieu)
- Employee → Employer: 30 days
- Both parties can mutually agree to waive notice period

**Termination Process:**
1. Issue written notice (30 days prior)
2. Final salary calculation (base + allowances + unused leave payout)
3. BPJS settlement (final contribution, benefit claims)
4. Return all company property
5. Release letter (Surat Keterangan Kerja) provided

**Final Payout Components:**
- Last month salary (prorated if mid-month): Full
- Severance: As per table above
- Service allowance: As per table above
- Unused annual leave: Payout at daily rate
- Performance bonus (if accrued): Per policy
- BPJS refund (if applicable): Lump-sum old age benefit

## 9. Payroll System Integration (Recommended Tools)

### 9.1 Technology Requirements

**For ElSpa Indonesia Operations, recommend:**

| Component | Tool/Approach | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Payroll Calculation** | Integrated HR/Payroll Software (e.g., Paychex, ADP, or local: Talenta, Jurnal) | Automates tax calculations, BPJS, compliance reporting |
| **Time Tracking** | Biometric (fingerprint/RFID) + mobile app | Accurate attendance records for wage deductions, overtime |
| **BPJS Integration** | Direct API to BPJS portal (if available) or manual monthly uploads | Seamless contribution reporting |
| **Tax Compliance** | DJP e-Bupot system connection (or accountant handles) | Automates PPh 21 filing |
| **Leave Management** | Custom module in payroll software | Tracks accrual, usage, carryover; calculates payout upon separation |
| **Reporting & Analytics** | Dashboard showing payroll trends, labor cost per therapist, turnover | Informs strategic decisions (pricing, staffing) |

### 9.2 Local Payroll Service Providers

**Option 1: DIY with Local Accountant**
- Engage Indonesian tax accountant (CPA equivalent)
- Accountant handles BPJS filing, tax reporting, compliance
- Cost: IDR 3–5M/month (depending on employee count)
- Pros: Cost-effective for small operations (<50 employees)
- Cons: Dependent on accountant; manual processes

**Option 2: Payroll Service Bureau (Indonesia-based)**
- Examples: Talenta, Jurnal (Indonesia), Paychex (regional)
- Full outsourced payroll: salary processing, tax filing, BPJS, reporting
- Cost: IDR 100k–500k per employee/month
- Pros: Modern software, compliance guaranteed, scalable
- Cons: Higher cost for small teams

**Option 3: Finance/Accounting Manager (In-house)**
- Hire local Finance Manager (IDR 12–18M/month)
- Finance Manager owns payroll operations, BPJS, tax compliance
- Cost: Salary + software license (IDR 5–10M/month)
- Pros: Full control, customizable processes, relationship management
- Cons: High fixed cost, requires hiring/training

**Recommendation for ElSpa:** 
- **Year 1 (0–20 employees):** Use payroll service bureau (low fixed cost, compliance-focused)
- **Year 2+ (20+ employees):** Hire in-house Finance Manager + payroll software

## 10. Key Compliance Risks & Mitigation

### 10.1 Common Non-Compliance Issues

| Risk | Consequence | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Below Minimum Wage** | Back pay + penalties (up to IDR 100M) + operational closure | Annual wage review; salary benchmarking against provincial minimum |
| **Missing BPJS Enrollment** | Penalties (2–5% of contribution amount) + employee disputes | Enroll within 30 days of hire; maintain enrollment records |
| **Late BPJS Contribution** | 2% penalty + interest on late payment | Automate payment; integrate with payroll system |
| **Incorrect PPh 21 Withholding** | Underpayment penalties; audit by tax authority | Use certified payroll software; monthly reconciliation with tax authority |
| **Unpaid Severance** | Lawsuit + labor court penalties (up to 2x severance amount) | Accrue severance monthly; legal review of termination procedures |
| **Missing Annual Leave Payout** | Employee legal claim + interest | Track leave accrual quarterly; include in separation checklist |
| **No Employment Contract** | Deemed indefinite employment; difficult to terminate; back pay claims | Provide written contract before first day; signed by both parties |

### 10.2 Audit Preparedness

**Documentation to Maintain (at least 5 years):**
- Employment contracts (all current & former employees)
- Timekeeping records (monthly)
- Payroll registers (monthly salary details)
- BPJS enrollment confirmations & contribution receipts
- Tax withholding reports (monthly PPh 21 filing confirmation)
- Leave records (accrual, usage, payout)
- Termination documentation (notice, settlement agreements)
- Performance evaluations (for any disciplinary actions)

**Audit Frequency:**
- Labor Department inspection: 1–2x per year (no fixed schedule)
- Tax Authority audit: Random, or triggered by discrepancies
- BPJS audit: Bi-annual (contribution reconciliation)

**Typical Inspection Focus:**
- Minimum wage compliance
- BPJS enrollment & contribution status
- Leave payment accuracy
- Overtime calculation
- Working hours compliance
